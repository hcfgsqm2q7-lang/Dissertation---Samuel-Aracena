## Work completed

- Conducted a literature review covering anticipatory action, food-security forecasting, political-violence forecasting and drought monitoring.
- Identified commonly used humanitarian features, including conflict, food prices, vegetation, rainfall, displacement, socioeconomic conditions and humanitarian response.
- Selected an admin-2 district/month as the proposed unit of analysis.
- Defined several proposed mechanisms and source datasets:

  - Conflict and insecurity using GDELT and another conflict source.
  - Climate and agricultural stress using CHIRPS rainfall.
  - Market and food-price pressure using WFP food-price data.
  - Economic conditions using World Bank and IMF indicators.
  - Humanitarian access and response using ReliefWeb reports.

- Considered alternative food-price measures, including commodity-level changes and country-specific food-basket indices.
- Identified limitations including reporting bias, inconsistent event taxonomies, geographic precision, national-level economic indicators and possible bias in food-basket construction.

## Progress and issues discussed

- It was considered too early to decide whether the final output should emphasise a dashboard, source agreement or another framing.
- The immediate requirement is to integrate the sources into a common dataset; the final framing should be informed by the resulting exploratory analysis.
- The proposed unit of analysis must be reconciled with sources having different temporal resolutions, such as quarterly or four-monthly data.
- A formal ontology and dataset schema are required.
- The same mechanism may be measured through multiple sources, allowing subsequent comparison between sources.

## Publication Potential

There is realistic publication potential, the project could become publishable if it produces three things:

1. A reproducible and well-documented admin-2/month panel integrating heterogeneous humanitarian sources.
2. A principled methodology for preserving provenance, granularity, missingness and uncertainty rather than merely flattening all sources into comparable columns.
3. An empirical finding about observability, complementarity or source dependence that matters beyond this particular dataset.

## Biggest Risks

1. The scientific question remains unresolved.
2. Excessive scope.
3. False harmonisation. A common schema can conceal conceptual incompatibility. GDELT event mentions, ACLED conflict incidents and ReliefWeb reports are not interchangeable measurements of the same latent event process.
4. Temporal leakage and pseudo-resolution. Lower-frequency data cannot simply be replicated across months without consequences.
5. Weak validation. The project may end with a dataset that looks plausible but has no convincing evidence of quality.
6. Licensing and reproducibility. An openly released dataset may not be legally possible if some source licences prohibit redistribution.

## Resolution

I would simplify the project around four mechanisms and five core sources.

The four mechanisms should be:

1. Conflict and insecurity.
2. Climate and agricultural stress.
3. Market and food-price pressure.
4. Humanitarian reporting and response.

The core sources should be:

1. IPC as the outcome;
2. ACLED for conflict;
3. CHIRPS for rainfall;
4. WFP Food Prices for market conditions;
5. ReliefWeb for humanitarian reporting.

GDELT can be retained as a second observation of conflict or reporting intensity because Samuel already has a pipeline. ICEWS, World Bank and IMF should be removed from the core version unless integration is trivial.

I would also drop national economic indicators from the first dataset release. Their mismatch with admin-2/month resolution is severe, and their scientific value is limited unless the project explicitly models macroeconomic shocks.
