By reading your last slide, the most promising direction to me seems to be:

> **Main RQ**. _How unevenly do humanitarian data sources observe different places and crisis mechanisms, and what are the consequences for food-insecurity analysis?_

The dataset is the method used to answer this question. A dashboard may be a useful output, but it should not become the main research contribution.

## Main Limitations

### 1. The research question is still not fixed

You are right that feature selection is difficult without a clear research question. At present, the project risks becoming a technically good integration exercise without a focused scientific conclusion.

I'd propose to frame the project around humanitarian observability. Examine which districts and mechanisms are well observed, which are poorly observed, and whether missing information is systematic rather than random.

A practical main question could be:

> How does the spatial and temporal observability of humanitarian conditions differ across conflict, climate, market and reporting sources?

Possible sub-questions are:

- Which mechanisms have the largest coverage gaps?
- Which districts are consistently poorly observed?
- Are market and reporting gaps associated with population, accessibility, urbanisation or conflict?
- Does combining sources reduce these observability gaps?

### 2. The number of features is not the main problem

Your explanation about the commodities is reasonable. Four commodities, each represented separately, can be justified if they are part of Somalia's relevant WFP basket. The problem is not that 21 features is automatically too many.

The real questions are whether the features are:

- theoretically justified;
- consistently available;
- comparable across countries;
- useful for the chosen analysis.

I propose to keep the commodity-specific features for the prototype, but classify them as a coherent market-feature family. Then evaluate their missingness, correlation and usefulness before deciding whether all should remain in the final release.


### 3. Commodity comparability may become difficult

The Somalia commodity basket may not transfer directly to Ethiopia, Kenya and South Sudan. Different commodities, units and consumption patterns may be relevant in each country.

I propose to keep country-specific commodity variables in the raw fact tables, but create a smaller harmonised layer for cross-country analysis. This could include measures such as:

- staple-food price anomaly;
- maximum commodity price anomaly;
- average anomaly across available staple commodities;
- number of commodities with unusually high prices.

This preserves detail without pretending that every commodity is directly comparable across countries.

### 4. Conflict density does not measure spatial distribution

Your motivation for retaining conflict density is understandable, but events divided by total district area does not directly measure the spatial distribution of conflict.

It mainly measures event concentration relative to administrative area. Small urban districts will tend to receive high values, while large sparsely populated districts will receive low values, even when the humanitarian exposure is similar.

The absence of reliable population data does not make area the correct denominator.

_Proposed solution_. Retain the feature temporarily as an exploratory variable, but rename it clearly as: `conflict_events_per_1000_km2`.

Do not call it a measure of humanitarian intensity or population exposure. Test whether it is largely explained by district area and urban status. Remove it from the final feature set if it does not add information beyond event counts.

To study spatial distribution more directly, consider measures such as:

- number of unique conflict locations within the district;
- proportion of district grid cells containing an event;
- average distance between events;
- concentration of events around the district's main settlement.

These are more direct spatial-distribution measures, although they may be unnecessary for this dissertation.

### 5. Some trend features remain unstable

Percentage change becomes undefined when the previous value is zero and becomes very large when the previous value is small. This affects conflict trends and potentially commodity-price trends.

_Proposed solution_. Document the zero-denominator rule and compare percentage change with more stable alternatives:

- log difference where values are positive;
- rolling mean or rolling slope;
- binary threshold increase/decrease indicators.

Use the simplest measure that has a clear interpretation.

### 6. ReliefWeb geoparsing is not yet validated

The current alias list covers only 16 of 74 districts, and 51% of reports match no district. This may create artificial differences between well-known districts and places with less recognisable names.

_Proposed solution_. Manually validate a sample of matched and unmatched reports. Report precision and recall, expand the alias dictionary and distinguish a genuine event location from a district mentioned only in passing.

### 7. Coverage is not the same as true observability

A complete Admin-2 table does not necessarily mean that the source fully observes every district. An ACLED zero may mean no reported event, not necessarily no conflict. Satellite coverage may be spatially complete but still have varying reliability.

I'd propose to separate three concepts:

1. _Structural availability_: can the source theoretically provide data there?
2. _Recorded coverage_: does the dataset contain an observation?
3. _Real-world observability_: how well does that observation represent actual conditions?

Use cautious language in the dissertation and slides.

### 8. Do not scale before validating the Somalia prototype

Scaling to four countries and ten years will introduce more naming conflicts, changing boundaries, currencies, commodities and missing-data patterns.

I'd propose to spend next week to make the Somalia pipeline scientifically defensible. Once the feature definitions, ReliefWeb validation and missingness analysis are complete, expand the time window. Add other countries only after confirming that the pipeline remains comparable.
