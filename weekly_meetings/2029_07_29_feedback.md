## Direction of the Research

The project is now moving towards building a research-ready humanitarian dataset rather than simply developing a prediction model. This is the right direction. The dataset itself should become the main contribution, while food insecurity prediction should only be used to demonstrate that the dataset is useful.

The next challenge is to prove that it works in practice.

## Publication Potential

The publication will depend on demonstrating that:

- the different datasets can actually be integrated successfully;
- the final dataset is reproducible and well documented;
- the integrated dataset reveals something scientifically interesting, such as source complementarity, observability or data quality.

Example of scientifically interesting findings (fictional examples to clarify what I mean):

> **Example 1**. Humanitarian reporting systematically under-represents drought-related crises compared with conflict-related crises.
>
> **Example 2**. Conflict information is consistently available at district level, whereas market and humanitarian response information are often available only at national level, creating systematic observability gaps.
>
> **Example 3**. Combining multiple sources increases spatial coverage from 42% to 91% of district-months.

At the moment, we have a strong design, but not yet strong empirical evidence.

## Major Risks and Limitations

- Too much time spent on design. You have developed a good conceptual framework, but now you need to start producing a working dataset.
- The prototype is still incomplete. This was the main task from last week and should now become the highest priority.
- ReliefWeb has a different spatial resolution. Most reports are country-level, making integration with Admin-2 data difficult.
- Some features need further justification. For example, conflict density based on area may not be meaningful (better to keep a simple conflict count, fatalities, violence against civilians count), and rainfall minimum/maximum may add little value (better rainfall anomaly with z-score).
- The feature set is becoming too large. A smaller number of robust features is preferable to many weak ones.
- Data quality has not yet been evaluated. You still need to measure missing values, spatial coverage and integration success across all sources.
- The scientific contribution is still not demonstrated. The dataset must eventually produce an interesting finding, not just a successful integration (see my previous point above).
